Musicking
Quadrant

So how can we think about all the ways we take part in music? I call it the musicking quadrant.

“To music is to take part, in any capacity, in a musical performance”

Christopher Small, Musicking (1998)

Anja has already introduced the term, so I will keep it short. Small goes on: whether by performing, by listening, by rehearsing or practising, by providing material for performance, what we call composing, or by dancing. He even includes the people who sell the tickets and those who clean up afterwards. Music is not an object but relationships between people. [ARJ: check the page number against the book.]
In my book Sound Actions (MIT Press, 2022), which is open access, I tried to sort musicking into what I call the musicking quadrant. The book is about musical instruments, but the quadrant is useful for thinking about musicking more generally.
(Click for the word.) Musicking can be sorted into four. (Click.) To create: the composer, the songwriter, the producer, the instrument maker. They shape the experience, but they do it before it happens. (Click.) To perform: the musician on stage, the DJ, the dancer. This happens in real time, in front of or together with others. (Click.) To experience: the audience, the listener, the dancer. This also happens in real time, and it is not passive: the body takes part. (Click.) To understand: the critic, the researcher, the teacher, the student. And the arrows close the circle: what we understand shapes what we create next. Most of us are in all four corners at different times in our lives.
Two axes. (Click.) One is time. Performing and experiencing happen in real time, now, in the room. Creating and understanding happen out of time: in the studio, at the desk, in the record shop. (Click.) The other runs from creation to experience. I deliberately avoid calling one active and the other passive: everyone is actively engaged, with the body.

Music(king)
as process

And this brings me to my main point: music is not only a thing, it is something we do.
This is my main point. (Click.) We are used to thinking about music as a product: a record, a file, a track in a playlist, something that can be bought, sold and owned. (Click.) Musicking frees us from that. It turns music into a process, something we do. (Click for each.) And then other things come into focus: embodiment, that we music with the whole body; enaction, that we make sense of music by acting; joint action, that we do it together, in time with each other; and social bonding, that music binds us together.

Embodiment

If music is something we do, the body is where it happens.
This builds on what is called 4E cognition. (Click.) Embodied: the mind is in the body, not only in the head. (Click.) Enactive: we understand the world by acting in it. (Click.) Embedded: we are always in a situation, a room, a culture. (Click.) Extended: our thinking reaches into the things we use, the instrument, the turntable, the phone. (Click.) Musicking covers all four; it is an excellent case for understanding how body and mind are connected. (Click.) And it says something about what kind of technology we should build: technology for both body and mind.
(Click.) Even a phone can be used for musicking: we play with the hands and the body, and the motion becomes sound. (Click.) We are many in the same room, (click) and the devices and the people connect with each other. Embodied and social, not one person alone with earphones.
A dance floor with a DJ, 2015 (my own photo). Here it is hard to say who performs and who experiences. The DJ plays other people's recordings and creates something new from them in real time. The dancers are the audience, but they are also the performance. In Norwegian we say without hesitation that we dance, while "musicking" is still a technical term. It should not be.

So what does it all add up to?

Music is
something we do.

Music is something we do, with the body, together. Over to Nanette.
That is why a day like this matters. Here today we have all four corners: the musicians and the labels creating, the DJ sets and the concerts, you experiencing, and the conversations that help us understand. (Click.) And they feed each other, in both directions. (Click.) Everything connects with everything. Small scenes keep the whole quadrant alive, even when the big platforms only want one corner.
And technology? (Click.) AI can sit in the middle and connect all four corners: helping us create, perform, experience and understand, instead of replacing one of them. (Click.) And it is not really about AI. It is about technology in general. Every musicking technology, from the piano to the turntable to the phone, can either close corners or open them. Our job is to build technologies that open them.
